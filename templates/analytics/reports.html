1 · Mamdani fuzzy system
Try it live
Four inputs (severity, public impact, waiting time, SLA urgency) → priority 0-100. Low and High use shoulder trapezoids, Medium a triangle, with ~50% overlap so the score changes smoothly. 20 rules, AND = min, clipped consequents, max aggregation, centroid defuzzification. Every grievance stores its fired-rule trace so the officer can see why it ranks where it does.

Reading the results honestly
  • All four methods process the same queues under the same capacity model. Queues are simulated from the category catalogue and deliberately overloaded, so absolute SLA-violation rates are high.
  • The GA beat the static-priority baseline on cost in {{ cmp.wins.ga_vs_baseline_cost }}/{{ cmp.wins.queues }} queues and on SLA violations in {{ cmp.wins.ga_vs_baseline_sla.0 }}/{{ cmp.wins.queues }} (ties {{ cmp.wins.ga_vs_baseline_sla.1 }}, losses {{ cmp.wins.ga_vs_baseline_sla.2 }}).
  • The cost function uses the fuzzy score, so cost favours fuzzy-aware methods; avg waiting time, SLA violations and runtime are the fairer columns.
  • The GA does not reduce the waiting time of high-priority cases compared with a plain fuzzy sort — it trades some of that for fewer SLA violations overall. The GA also costs ~0.2 s per queue versus ~0 s for sorting.
3 · Genetic algorithm design
Chromosome
A permutation of the pending grievances; the schedule is simulated with list scheduling over the department's officers.
Fitness
Minimise priority-weighted waiting + SLA-violation penalty + extra delay for high-priority cases + capacity overflow; invalid chromosomes get a huge penalty.
Operators
Tournament selection (3), order crossover (OX), swap/inversion mutation, elitism (2), stop after 150 generations or 30 without improvement.
